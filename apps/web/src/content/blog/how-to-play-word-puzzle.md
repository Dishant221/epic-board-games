Word Puzzle is the game you can explain in one breath and still be thinking about at bedtime. There is a hidden five-letter word. You have six guesses. After each guess the tiles turn green, yellow or grey to tell you how close you were. That is all, and yet every round is a tiny detective story. This guide explains the rules precisely, walks through a full game, and shows how to turn a solo puzzle into a friendly daily ritual.

## What you need

A browser and about five minutes. Word Puzzle is a single-player game with no account required, though signing in lets you keep your streak alongside your other games. It works on phones, tablets and desktops, with an on-screen keyboard that doubles as your notebook.

## Setup

Open the game and you will see an empty grid of six rows and five columns, plus the keyboard beneath it. The hidden word has already been chosen. Type a five-letter word and press Enter to make your first guess.

## All the rules

- **Guess the hidden five-letter word in six tries.** Every guess must be a real word; the game will not accept random letters, so you cannot "probe" with ASDFG.
- **Green** means the letter is correct and in the right position.
- **Yellow** means the letter is in the hidden word but in a different position.
- **Grey** means the letter is not in the hidden word at all.
- **The on-screen keyboard colours track what you have learned.** Letters you have proved wrong turn grey on the keyboard; letters you have found turn yellow or green. You never have to remember which letters you have already tried.
- If your sixth guess is wrong, the hidden word is revealed and the round is over.

## Repeated letters

The one rule that trips up newcomers is how repeated letters are coloured. The usual convention in games of this kind, and the one to expect here, is that a letter is marked only as many times as it appears in the answer. Suppose the hidden word is ROBOT and you guess MOTTO. The first T is yellow (ROBOT contains one T, and it is not in position 3) but the second T is grey, because the answer has no second T to match. Green matches are allocated first, then yellows from left to right. If a double letter ever surprises you, read the row again with this convention in mind; it usually explains the colours.

## A worked game

Here is a real round, hidden word STORM, to show how the colours drive your thinking.

1. **CRANE.** C, A, N and E all come back grey; R is yellow. Four letters eliminated in one go, and we know R is somewhere other than position 2.
2. **SLOTH.** S turns green in position 1, O green in position 3, T yellow in position 4, L and H grey. The shape is now S _ O _ _ with R and T still to place, and T cannot be fourth.
3. **STORK.** S, T, O and R all green; K grey. Only the final letter is wrong.
4. **STORM.** All green. Solved in four with two guesses to spare.

Notice the pattern: the first guess is about *eliminating*, the middle guesses are about *positioning*, and the last guess is about *finishing*. Our strategy post on [starting words and letter frequency](/blog/word-puzzle-starting-words-letter-frequency) goes deep on that first step.

## Playing with a little discipline

Some players enjoy a self-imposed "hard mode": every guess must use all the green and yellow letters you have already found. It forces you to commit to what you know rather than throwing away a guess on pure elimination. It is not enforced by the game, so agree the rule with yourself (or your rivals) before you start. Playing this way makes the colours matter more and tends to produce more dramatic last-row finishes.

## Strategy tips for beginners

- **Open with common letters.** Words rich in E, A, R, O, T, S and L reveal the most. CRANE, SLATE and TRACE are popular for good reason.
- **Do not reuse grey letters.** The keyboard is already telling you they are dead.
- **Move yellows around.** If R was yellow in position 2, your next guess should try R somewhere else.
- **Count the vowels.** Every five-letter word has at least one; if your first two guesses find none of A, E, I or O, think about U and Y.
- **Watch for word endings.** -ER, -ED, -LY, -ES and -ST finish a huge share of five-letter words.

## Common mistakes

- Spending a guess on a word with letters you have already proved grey.
- Repeating a letter in an early guess, which wastes a tile that could have tested a new letter.
- Forgetting that a yellow letter cannot sit where you just tried it.
- Panicking on the last row: list every word that fits the greens before you type.

## Playing with friends and family

Word Puzzle is solo on screen but wonderfully social off it. The classic ritual is to play the same word on separate devices and compare only your *number of guesses*, never the letters, so nobody is spoiled. Share your coloured grid with the words hidden and let friends admire (or mock) your path to the answer. On one device, take turns typing guesses and discussing the logic together; children pick up letter patterns remarkably fast when they can see the keyboard changing colour. For a longer evening, play a best-of-five and award points for every guess you did not need.
