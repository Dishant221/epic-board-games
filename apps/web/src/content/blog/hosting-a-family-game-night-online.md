A good game night has three ingredients: people who want to be there, games that fit the group, and as little faff as possible between "hello" and the first roll. Playing online adds a fourth: everyone has to be able to find the same table. This guide is the checklist we use ourselves. It covers accounts and friends, the difference between matchmaking and private rooms, voice and text chat, choosing games by age and time, and a 90-minute schedule you can copy tonight.

## Before the night: accounts and friends

Every game on Epic Board Games is free, and you can play against the AI without signing in. To play **with** other people you need a free account, because the game has to know who you are to put you in the same room. Ask everyone to [sign up](/signup) a day early; it takes a minute, and it avoids the classic game-night opening of three people reading a password reset email aloud.

Once signed in, open the [Friends page](/friends) and add each other. Friends show as **online** when they are on the site, and you can **ping** a friend to invite them straight into the room you are in. For a family group, one person should be the host: they add everyone, create the rooms and keep the evening moving.

## Two ways to fill a table

### Private rooms

From any game's page, choose the option to **create a private room and invite friends or family**. You get a room that only invited players can join. Ping your friends and they appear in the lobby; when everyone is in, the host starts the game. Private rooms are the right choice for a family night because there are no strangers and no clock: the game waits until you say go.

### Online Multiplayer matchmaking

If you are short of players, or the kids want a game while the adults are still making tea, choose **Online Multiplayer** instead. Matchmaking puts you in a room with other players looking for the same game. The game starts the moment the room is full (up to four players depending on the game), or **after 60 seconds if at least two players have joined**, so you are never left waiting in an empty lobby.

### Mixing in the AI

Any empty seat in a private room can be filled with an AI player. This is useful when you have three humans and a four-player game, or when a young child wants to "play against the robot" while an adult helps. For a family night, pick **Buddy** or **Nova** so the computer does not run away with the game.

## Voice chat and text chat

Voice chat is built into every multiplayer room; there is no separate app to install. The first time you use it, your browser asks for **microphone permission**; allow it and you will hear each other immediately. Phones, tablets and laptops all work, and a pair of earbuds avoids echo when two people are in the same room at home.

Text chat runs alongside, with **safe-chat filters** that catch language you would not want a nine-year-old to read, and a set of quick **emotes** for the moments when typing is too slow. If a child is playing with people outside the family, you can rely on the filters; if the whole room is family, voice is more fun.

## Choosing games by age and time

The catalogue lists a typical length for every game. Use it to build an evening that fits the group:

| Game | Players | Typical length | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Tic Tac Toe](/games/tic-tac-toe) | 2 | 2 min | Warm-up, very young children |
| [Connect 4](/games/connect-4) | 2 | 5 min | Quick duels, ages 6+ |
| [Snakes, Dragons & Ladders](/games/snakes-and-ladders) | 2–4 | 10 min | Whole-family races, pure luck |
| [Checkers](/games/checkers) | 2 | 15 min | Thinkers, ages 8+ |
| [Epic Puzzle Board](/games/epic-puzzle) | 1–2 | 15 min | Winding down, co-op |
| [Chess](/games/chess) | 2 | 30 min | Older players, one long game |
| [Epic Business](/games/epic-business) | 2–4 | 45 min | The main event with teens and adults |

A few principles:

- **Start short.** Two-minute games absorb late arrivals and microphone problems without anyone feeling they missed out.
- **Put the four-player game in the middle**, when everyone has arrived and nobody has left yet.
- **Finish cooperatively.** Ending on a puzzle rather than a contest sends everyone to bed in a good mood.
- **Match the game to the youngest player**, then give older players the harder AI or a side game of Chess.

## A sample 90-minute evening

1. **0–5 min: Join and mic check.** Host creates a private Snakes room, pings everyone, and you all say hello on voice chat.
2. **5–15 min: Tic Tac Toe warm-up.** Pairs rotate through quick games while stragglers arrive.
3. **15–35 min: Snakes, Dragons & Ladders, two races.** Four players, winner of race one picks the pawn colours for race two.
4. **35–45 min: Break.** Snacks, bathroom, and someone finds a charger.
5. **45–70 min: Two tables of two.** Connect 4 for the younger pair, Checkers for the older pair; swap after the first game.
6. **70–90 min: Epic Puzzle Board together.** One shared canvas, everyone on voice chat, last piece to the youngest.

If you have older players and a longer evening, replace steps 3 to 5 with a single game of Epic Business.

## Troubleshooting

- **Nobody can hear me.** Check the browser's microphone permission (look for the padlock or site-settings icon in the address bar) and make sure another app is not using the mic. Refresh the page if you allowed permission after the room started.
- **I can't see the room.** Make sure you are signed in and that the host has pinged you; the invite appears as a notification.
- **The game froze or I lost connection.** Refresh the page and rejoin from the ping or the game page; turn-based games pick up where they left off.
- **The matchmaking room started without my cousin.** Matchmaking starts after 60 seconds once two players are present. Use a private room when you want to wait for specific people.
- **It's hard to see on a phone.** Rotate to landscape for board games; portrait is fine for Connect 4 and the puzzle tray.
