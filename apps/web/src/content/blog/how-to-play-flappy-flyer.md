Flappy Flyer is the odd one out in our collection. There are no turns, no opponents and no dice. It is a **realtime arcade game**: the moment you start, gravity starts too, and the only thing between you and the ground is your timing. One tap makes the Flyer flap. Everything else is nerve.

It is also the game most likely to produce a shout from the next room. Here is how it works, how to stop crashing into the second pipe, and how to turn it into a competition the whole household can join on a single phone.

## What you need

One player and one input: a finger on a touchscreen, a mouse button or the space bar. A round lasts anything from two seconds to a couple of minutes depending on how good you are, so a session is really a stack of attempts. Your best score is saved on the device you are playing on, so the leaderboard you are chasing is your own.

## Setup

There is nothing to set up. Open the game, and the Flyer hovers in a ready state until your first tap. The first tap both starts the round and gives you your first flap, so be ready to tap again almost immediately: the Flyer begins falling the instant the round starts.

## How the controls work

### Flap

Tap the screen, click, or press space. Each input gives the Flyer a single upward push. The push is the same size every time: you cannot flap harder by pressing longer or tapping twice quickly, you simply flap twice.

### Gravity

Between flaps, gravity pulls the Flyer down and keeps accelerating it. That is why the flight path is a series of arcs rather than a straight line. A flap stops the fall, sends the Flyer up for a moment, and then gravity takes over again. The longer you wait, the faster you are falling when you finally tap, and the lower the arc bottoms out.

### Pipes

Pairs of pipes scroll towards you from the right. Each pair has a gap, and the gap's height varies from pair to pair. You score one point for every pair you pass through.

## All the rules

1. **Tap, click or press space to flap.** Each input is one flap of equal strength.
2. **Gravity pulls you down between flaps.** The longer between taps, the faster you fall.
3. **Fly through the gap in each pair of pipes to score a point.**
4. **Touching a pipe or the ground ends the round.** The top of the screen is not forgiving either: flying too high while a pipe arrives is the most common way to crash.
5. **Your best score is saved on this device.** Beat it and the game tells you.

## Technique

### Aim for the lower third of the gap

![Flappy Flyer pipe gap with the safe aim zone shaded in the lower third](/blog/flappy-gap.svg)

New players aim for the middle of the gap. The problem is that a flap lifts the Flyer *up*, so if you are in the middle when you tap, you rise towards the top pipe. Aim instead for the **lower third** of the gap. A flap there carries you up through the gap and gravity brings you back down on the far side. You clear the lower lip because you are rising, and you clear the upper lip because you started low.

### Find a rhythm

![Flappy Flyer flight path showing an even tap rhythm compared with tap spamming](/blog/flappy-tap-rhythm.svg)

The Flyer stays level if you tap at the bottom of each dip with an even rhythm. Try counting under your breath. When you need to climb, tap slightly earlier; to descend, wait slightly longer. What you should never do is spam the tap: each flap adds another push, and three quick taps will fire you straight into the top pipe.

### Look at the next pipe, not the Flyer

Your eyes should be a little ahead of the Flyer, reading where the next gap is. The Flyer's own position is predictable; the gap's position is what changes. Experienced players describe a kind of tunnel vision where the Flyer disappears and only the gaps remain.

### Small corrections early

If the next gap is higher than the current one, start adjusting as soon as you leave the current gap. A single extra tap early is far safer than two frantic taps late.

## Common mistakes

- **Tapping on the way up.** Wait until the Flyer has started to fall again before the next flap.
- **Aiming at the middle of the gap.** Aim low; the flap does the rising.
- **Freezing after a near miss.** A scare makes people stop tapping, and stopping tapping means falling. Keep the rhythm.
- **Playing too long when tired.** Reaction games reward short, fresh sessions. If your score is dropping, take a break.
- **Changing input device mid-session.** Thumb, mouse and space bar all have slightly different latency. Pick one and stick with it for the session.

## Touch, mouse or keyboard?

All three work, and the right one is whichever feels most consistent to you. Touchscreens are popular because the thumb rests directly on the surface and there is no travel. The space bar gives a very firm, repeatable press and suits people who learned on a keyboard. A mouse click is fine but adds a tiny bit of motion. If you are serious about a personal best, play on the same device every time so that the saved score and your muscle memory stay in step.

## Playing with friends and family

Flappy Flyer is single player with no AI, no matchmaking and no online rooms. That does not stop it being a great social game; it just moves the competition into the room.

- **Pass the phone.** Everyone gets three attempts, highest score wins. Keep a running tally on paper or in a group chat.
- **Score ladder.** Set a target (ten pipes, then twenty) and see who reaches it first.
- **Commentary rule.** The person waiting their turn provides commentary. This makes people worse at the game and much better at laughing.
- **Device championships.** Because the best score is saved per device, each phone or tablet in the house keeps its own record. Who holds the family tablet's high score becomes a matter of honour.

For a shared online experience, pair a Flappy Flyer score battle with voice chat in one of our multiplayer games, or simply swap screenshots of your best round.
